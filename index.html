Research

Undergraduate Thesis · BRAC University · 2024

Enhancing Bangla Text Summarization in a Monolingual Setting

Abstractive Bangla-to-Bangla summarization is under-studied: the language is morphologically rich, resources are scarce, and generated summaries often repeat themselves, drift off topic or drop key facts. This thesis builds a larger dataset, benchmarks several model families on it, and examines how and why they fail.

22.5Karticle–summary pairs in the extended dataset
5model families compared
8+evaluation metrics beyond ROUGE

Dataset

Started from the Bangla portion of XL-Sum (about 10K BBC pairs) and extended it with article–summary pairs collected from The Daily Star, reaching 22,531 samples across more than one news source. Preprocessing removed nulls, duplicates and pairs whose text was not at least twice the summary length. An intrinsic evaluation (abstractivity, compression, novel n-grams, redundancy, BERTScore, BLEU) showed the merged data keeps the character of the original, for example abstractivity of 71.6% versus 72.8%.

Models

Fine-tuned mT5, BanglaT5, mBART and Bloom-576M using PyTorch and Hugging Face Transformers. Llama 3.1 was evaluated with prompt engineering (LangChain) and 4-bit quantization rather than fine-tuning, because of compute limits.

Evaluation

ROUGE-1/2/L and BERTScore for overlap and semantics, plus abstractivity, compression, novel n-gram and redundancy metrics, since ROUGE alone penalizes valid paraphrases in a language with many synonyms and rich morphology.

Key findings

  • mT5 was the strongest overall, with ROUGE-1 of 21.0 and the best BERTScore F1 (74.7%) on the held-out set.
  • BanglaT5 produced the most abstractive summaries (62.7% abstractivity, highest novel n-grams), trading some ROUGE for more rewording.
  • Bloom-576M generated passages rather than summaries, which both ROUGE and the compression metric exposed.
  • Manual error analysis grouped failures into length mismatch, unrelated or hallucinated content, missing key information, incomplete outputs and repetition.

Status: completed as an undergraduate thesis; not yet published. I plan to extend it with more domains, manual annotation and larger-scale fine-tuning for submission.
